Efficient Systems for LLMs (Inference and Training)

This is the core research area at NEXT Lab. The agenda focuses on end-to-end system design for large language models across client, edge, and accelerator-rich environments, combining runtime innovation, architecture co-design, and deployment-driven evaluation.

Core Research Areas

1) Efficient Systems for LLMs (Inference and Training)

Runtime systems and kernel methods for memory-efficient inference, low-latency serving, and efficient training/fine-tuning under realistic power and bandwidth constraints.

2) Domain-Specific Architecture (DSA) for Scientific Computing

Domain-specialized architecture and accelerator co-design for scientific workloads, with algorithm-hardware alignment for scalable performance.

3) Mixed-Signal Circuit Design for Sensing Systems

Analog, digital, and optical circuit/system design for sensing pipelines that bridge device-level innovation and system-level intelligence.

Representative LLM Systems Themes

  • Memory-centric execution for long-context and generative workloads.
  • Quantization- and bandwidth-aware kernel generation for NPUs.
  • Runtime systems for predictable latency, throughput, and power envelopes in inference and training.
  • Cross-layer evaluation spanning model behavior, compiler/runtime, and accelerator architecture.

Evidence of Impact

The lab's LLM systems direction is reinforced by external adoption signals, sustained federal/industry funding, and reproducible cross-layer artifacts spanning software and architecture.
